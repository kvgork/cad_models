# finray_tests v2: one base, swappable actuation modules (GRIP-9017)

X1, not printed yet. **NEEDS PRINT TEST.** The v1 thumb-screw rig, rebuilt so the same fin-ray fingers can be driven in different ways. The base and the drive stay (knob, M6 screw, rack and pinion, two carriages that move symmetrically). Each carriage now carries a module instead of a finger, screwed onto two heat-set inserts in its top. Swap the module, keep the fingers.

**Stage 1 (user, 2026-11-17):** finger design A only, two modules: **parallel** and **pull**. The angular module (rotating root), the back-face pusher and the other four rib layouts (B, C, A+15, A−15) follow after the first print results. The rig is driven by hand; a servo can drive the M6 screw later.

![parallel module, top](docs/build/step5_parallel-done-top.png)

## How the modules move the finger

| Module | Holds the root | What the knob does | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Parallel** (mounted in the CAD) | Both root corners pinned in one holder on the carriage | The whole finger moves toward the other one, as in v1 | grip faces 2 … 70 mm apart, 1 mm per jaw per knob turn |
| **Pull** (parked behind the base in the CAD) | Outer corner pinned in a bracket screwed to the base; inner corner pinned in a slider | Pulls the inner (grip-side) corner back along the finger while the outer corner stays: the fin ray curls toward the object | 0 … 6 mm, 1 mm per knob turn |

**The new finger root.** v1's fingers had a 5 mm solid root block and a dovetail tongue. Here the block is gone: the grip wall and the back wall each end in a corner boss (Ø7, Ø3.4 pin hole), and the two bosses are joined only through the ribs. That is what lets the pull module bend the finger instead of tilting it (critic review, 2026-11-17). In the parallel module the holder clamps both bosses at a fixed spacing, so the holder is the fin ray's rigid base. The bosses turn on their pins, so the flank ends act as hinges rather than being clamped. The finger may therefore be a little softer than v1's at the root: compare A here with v1 A in the same test.

**Pull module, how it works.** A cam plate on each carriage has a 45° slot over the slider's drive boss. Closing the knob moves the carriage 1 mm toward the centre and the slot pushes the slider 1 mm back along the finger. The slot ends (1 mm past zero and past 6 mm) are the stops: **don't force the knob past them.** The M6 screw turns a little knob torque into several hundred N. The inner pin hole in the slider is a 3 mm slot across the finger, so the root can narrow as the ribs tilt.

**Setting the gap in pull mode.** The fingers don't travel in this mode: the brackets set the gap. Each bracket has two 10 mm slots over a row of M3 nuts under the base (every 10 mm, x = ±15 … ±55), so any finger gap from 10 to 60 mm can be set:
1. Turn the knob until both sliders sit against the zero-pull end of their slot.
2. Loosen the bracket screws, slide both brackets (with the carriages) to the gap you want, the same distance from the centre on both sides, and tighten.
3. Count knob turns from there: 1 turn = 1 mm of pull.

**Back-face pusher (stage 2).** It is kept as a comparison test. A fin ray bends toward a load on its flank, so pressing the back face bulges the middle of the grip face toward the object but swings the tip away: a convex contact, not a wrap.

## Testing

Same procedure as v1 (squeeze sweep, contact point, hold with a luggage scale, sideways and twist pulls, wrap photo; see [v1](../v1_parallel/README.md#the-fin-ray-designs)). Between modules, compare at equal **tip closure** (measure the tip movement), not at equal knob turns: 1 turn means 1 mm of jaw travel in the parallel module and 1 mm of root pull in the pull module, and these bend the finger differently.

## Build plan

Sorting sheet with 1:1 outlines: [`docs/GRIP-9017_assy-finray-multi-actuation_sort-sheet_A4.pdf`](docs/GRIP-9017_assy-finray-multi-actuation_sort-sheet_A4.pdf). New parts are orange in each picture.

**Drive (both modules)**

1. **Base and pinion.** Pinion on the post, then the M3 washer and the M3×8 screw on the post top. Push 4 M3 nuts into the pockets under the base where the pull brackets will go (x = ±35 and ±45 for a 40 mm gap; any two pockets 10 mm apart).
   ![step 1](docs/build/step1_base-pinion.png)
2. **Carriages, screw and knob.** Melt two M3 heat-set inserts into the top of each carriage first. M6 nut into jaw A's slot from the top. Slide both carriages onto the rail from the open end (+X), A first, and time them the same distance from the centre before the racks reach the pinion. Press the M6×70 head into the knob, push it through the wall, nyloc nut on (snug), screw into jaw A.
   ![step 2](docs/build/step2_carriages-screw-knob.png)

**Parallel module**

3. **Holders.** One holder on each carriage, lip against the carriage front, 2 × M3×8 each. The two holders are the same part.
   ![step 3](docs/build/step3_parallel-holders.png)
4. **Fingers and pins.** Slide the finger's two root bosses between the holder's floor and cap from the front, then push a snap pin down through cap, boss and floor at each corner until the barb clicks under the floor. The left finger is the right one turned over.
   ![step 4](docs/build/step4_parallel-fingers-pins.png)
5. **Done.**
   ![step 5](docs/build/step5_parallel-done-top.png)

**Pull module** (take the holders off; keep the fingers and pins)

1. **Brackets.** Screw each bracket to the base through its two slots into the nuts (M3×8), loosely; left and right brackets are mirror parts (L/R in the name).
   ![pull 1](docs/build/pull1_brackets.png)
2. **Sliders.** Drop each slider into its bracket's guide, drive boss up. The two sliders are the same part.
   ![pull 2](docs/build/pull2_sliders.png)
3. **Fingers and pins.** The outer boss goes in the bracket's fork, the inner boss in the slider's fork; a snap pin through each.
   ![pull 3](docs/build/pull3_fingers-pins.png)
4. **Cam plates.** Turn the knob until the slider bosses sit under the zero-pull end of the slots, then screw the cam plates onto the carriages (M3×12 in the counterbores). Set the gap and tighten the brackets (see above).
   ![pull 4](docs/build/pull4_cam-plates.png)
5. **Done.**
   ![pull 5](docs/build/pull5_done-top.png)

| M6 screw, nyloc, nut, knob at both stroke ends | no overlap | |

Fixes the checks led to: the inner pin's barb hit the bracket block at 3 mm of pull (groove cut in the bracket); the pin heads passed under the cam plate (plate raised over them); the slider-vs-cam-plate sweep was missing at first (added). The slicer then flagged the right bracket and the finger on the plate (print faces made flush, finger laid flat).

**Not checked:**
- **How the fingers bend.** There is no FEA. In the pull variant the finger is held rigid on the bracket, so the inner pin moving inside its boss is expected and left out of the sweeps; the pull test answers whether the fin ray curls the way it should.
- How hard the screw can push: there are no end stops other than the slot ends and the rail (the M6 screw gives a large force).
- The M3 screws of the cam plates and the heat-set inserts themselves are not modelled; the holder and bracket screw heads are.
- Rail friction, TPU swelling, pin wear.
